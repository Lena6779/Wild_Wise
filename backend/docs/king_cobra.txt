King Cobra

Sources: This fact sheet was summarized from https://www.nationalgeographic.com/animals/reptiles/facts/king-cobra (National Geographic).

Animal class: The king cobra is a reptile, and it is the longest venomous snake in the world. Scientists now think there are four kinds of king cobras.

Birth: King cobras lay eggs. King cobras are the only snakes in the world that build nests for their eggs, and they guard the nest fiercely until the babies hatch.

Diet: King cobras are carnivores that mostly eat other snakes, both venomous and nonvenomous, and sometimes even other king cobras. King cobras also eat lizards, eggs, and small mammals. The king cobra's scientific name means "snake-eater" in Greek.

Habitat: King cobras live mainly in the rain forests and plains of India, southern China, and Southeast Asia. King cobras live in forests, bamboo thickets, mangrove swamps, high grasslands, and near rivers.

Hibernation or migration: The source for this fact sheet does not say whether king cobras hibernate or migrate.

Fun facts: King cobras can grow up to 18 feet long, weigh up to 20 pounds, and live about 20 years in the wild. When a king cobra feels threatened, it spreads its hood, lifts up to a third of its body off the ground, and hisses with a sound like a growling dog. One king cobra bite has enough venom to kill 20 people or even an elephant, but king cobras are shy and usually avoid people. King cobras cannot hear most sounds in the air and instead feel vibrations in the ground.
