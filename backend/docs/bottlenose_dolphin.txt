Bottlenose Dolphin

Sources: This fact sheet was summarized from https://kids.nationalgeographic.com/animals/mammals/bottlenose-dolphin/ (National Geographic Kids) and https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/species/common-bottlenose-dolphin (NOAA Fisheries).

Animal class: The common bottlenose dolphin is a mammal that lives in the ocean.

Birth: Bottlenose dolphins give birth to live young, and calves are born tail-first. A mother bottlenose dolphin is pregnant for about 12 months, nurses her calf for about 20 months, and keeps it with her for 3 to 6 years.

Diet: Bottlenose dolphins are carnivores that eat fish, squid, and shrimp. Some groups of bottlenose dolphins work together to stir up a ring of mud that traps fish.

Habitat: Bottlenose dolphins live in warm waters all over the world, both in shallow water close to shore and in deep water far offshore.

Hibernation or migration: Bottlenose dolphins travel in groups that often split up and come back together. NOAA Fisheries tracks one group of bottlenose dolphins along the U.S. Atlantic coast that it calls a migratory coastal stock.

Fun facts: Each bottlenose dolphin makes its own special whistle that works like a name. Bottlenose dolphins use echolocation, sending out clicks and listening to the echoes to find objects. Bottlenose dolphins can leap as high as 20 feet and swim up to 22 miles an hour.
