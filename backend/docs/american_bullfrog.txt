American Bullfrog

Sources: This fact sheet was summarized from https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/american-bullfrogs-fast-facts.htm (National Park Service).

Animal class: The American bullfrog is an amphibian, and it is the largest frog in the United States.

Birth: American bullfrogs lay eggs. A female American bullfrog lays a floating, jelly-like mass of 12,000 to 20,000 eggs on the surface of the water. American bullfrog eggs hatch into tadpoles, which usually live in the water through two winters before they become frogs.

Diet: American bullfrogs are carnivores. American bullfrogs eat crayfish, water beetles, snails, dragonfly nymphs, small fish, and sometimes small birds.

Habitat: American bullfrogs live in ponds, where their green and brown colors help them blend in with pond plants.

Hibernation or migration: The source for this fact sheet does not say whether adult American bullfrogs hibernate or migrate. American bullfrog tadpoles stay in the water for usually two winters.

Fun facts: Only male American bullfrogs croak, making a deep "jug-o-rum" call to attract mates and claim territory. Male American bullfrogs have yellow throats, and females have white throats. American bullfrogs can grow up to 8 inches long, weigh over a pound, and live about 8 years. American bullfrogs can take in oxygen through their wet skin.
