Emperor Penguin

Sources: This fact sheet was summarized from https://kids.nationalgeographic.com/animals/birds/emperor-penguin/ (National Geographic Kids).

Animal class: The emperor penguin is a bird that cannot fly.

Birth: Emperor penguins lay eggs. A female emperor penguin lays a single egg in winter and then goes to sea, while the father keeps the egg warm on his feet under a fold of feathered skin for about 65 days.

Diet: Emperor penguins are carnivores that catch their food in the sea. Emperor penguin parents bring food back up from their stomachs to feed their chicks.

Habitat: Emperor penguins spend their whole lives on the ice of Antarctica and in the cold waters around it.

Hibernation or migration: Emperor penguins do not migrate far from Antarctica, but they make long trips to find food. A single emperor penguin feeding trip can cover up to 620 miles (1,000 kilometers), and mothers return from the sea after about two months to take over feeding the chick.

Fun facts: Emperor penguins can stand up to 45 inches tall and weighing up to 88 pounds. Emperor penguin chicks wait together in groups called creches while their parents fish. By December, emperor penguin chicks are ready to swim and fish on their own.
