Field Cricket

Sources: This fact sheet was summarized from https://mdc.mo.gov/discover-nature/field-guide/field-crickets (Missouri Department of Conservation).

Animal class: The field cricket is an insect, related to grasshoppers and katydids.

Birth: Field crickets lay eggs. A female field cricket lays her eggs underground using a long tube at the end of her body. Field cricket eggs hatch into nymphs, which look like small adults with tiny wing buds and molt several times before they grow full wings.

Diet: Field crickets are omnivores. Field crickets eat leaves, fruit, grain, other insects, and meat.

Habitat: Field crickets live in lawns, fields, pastures, prairies, roadsides, and woods, and they sometimes come into buildings. The house cricket probably came from Europe and Asia but now lives almost everywhere in the world.

Hibernation or migration: Most field crickets survive the winter as eggs underground, and the eggs hatch in spring.

Fun facts: Only male field crickets chirp. Male field crickets chirp by rubbing rough parts of their wings together to attract females and warn other males away. Field crickets are active at night, which is why people hear them chirping after dark. Field crickets are usually about half an inch to 1 inch long.
