Dragonfly

Sources: This fact sheet was summarized from https://www.nationalgeographic.com/animals/invertebrates/facts/dragonflies (National Geographic).

Animal class: The dragonfly is an insect. There are about 7,000 kinds of true dragonflies alive today.

Birth: Dragonflies lay eggs. A female dragonfly lays her eggs in fresh water around midsummer by dipping the end of her body into it, and the eggs hatch in about seven to eight days. Young dragonflies, called nymphs, live underwater for up to three years and molt 6 to 15 times before becoming adults.

Diet: Dragonflies are carnivores that eat other insects. Adult dragonflies catch flies, mosquitoes, and midges in the air, while dragonfly nymphs eat water insects, mosquito larvae, and small fish.

Habitat: Dragonflies live on every continent except Antarctica. Dragonflies need fresh water such as ponds and bogs, because their nymphs grow up underwater.

Hibernation or migration: The source for this fact sheet does not say whether dragonflies hibernate or migrate.

Fun facts: Dragonflies can fly up to 35 miles an hour, fly backward, and lift straight up like a helicopter. Each of a dragonfly's huge eyes is made of about 28,000 tiny lenses. Dragonflies have been around for about 325 million years. Adult dragonflies usually live only about a month.
