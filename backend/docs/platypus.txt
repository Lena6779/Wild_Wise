Platypus

Sources: This fact sheet was summarized from https://www.nationalgeographic.com/animals/mammals/facts/platypus (National Geographic) and https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Ornithorhynchus_anatinus (Animal Diversity Web, University of Michigan).

Animal class: The platypus is a mammal, and it is one of only two kinds of mammals that lay eggs. The other egg-laying mammal is the echidna.

Birth: Platypuses lay eggs instead of giving birth to live young. A female platypus seals herself inside her burrow and lays one or two eggs, which she keeps warm between her body and tail until they hatch in about ten days. Baby platypuses are about the size of lima beans, and the mother nurses them with milk for three to four months.

Diet: Platypuses are carnivores that feed on the bottom of rivers and streams, scooping up insects, insect larvae, shellfish, and worms along with gravel and mud. Platypuses have no teeth, so the gravel helps them mash their food.

Habitat: Platypuses live only in Australia, in the wetter parts of eastern Australia and on the island of Tasmania. Platypuses hunt underwater and dig burrows at the water's edge.

Hibernation or migration: The sources for this fact sheet do not say whether platypuses hibernate or migrate.

Fun facts: The first scientists to see a platypus thought it was a hoax. A platypus has a bill and webbed feet like a duck, a tail like a beaver, and a body and fur like an otter. Male platypuses are venomous, with sharp spurs on their back feet. A platypus can stay underwater for a minute or two.
