Great Horned Owl

Sources: This fact sheet was summarized from https://www.allaboutbirds.org/guide/Great_Horned_owl/lifehistory (Cornell Lab of Ornithology) and https://nwf.org/Educational-Resources/Wildlife-Guide/Birds/Great-Horned-Owl (National Wildlife Federation).

Animal class: The great horned owl is a bird of prey, also called a raptor.

Birth: Great horned owls lay eggs. A female great horned owl lays 1 to 4 dull white, nearly round eggs once a year, and the eggs hatch after 30 to 37 days. Great horned owl chicks stay in the nest for about 42 days.

Diet: Great horned owls are carnivores with the most varied diet of any North American raptor. Great horned owls mostly eat mammals and birds, such as rabbits, hares, mice, and American coots, and they also eat reptiles, insects, fish, skunks, and geese.

Habitat: Great horned owls live all across North America up to the northern tree line and in much of Central and South America. Great horned owls live in forests, woodlands, swamps, orchards, farmland, deserts, wooded parks, suburbs, and even cities.

Hibernation or migration: The sources for this fact sheet do not say whether great horned owls hibernate or migrate. Great horned owls are nocturnal and hunt at night, though they sometimes hunt in daylight in winter.

Fun facts: The "horns" on a great horned owl are really tufts of feathers used for territorial displays, and its ears are hidden holes under its feathers. Great horned owls have soft-edged flight feathers that let them fly silently. Great horned owls sometimes line their nests with feathers from their own chests.
