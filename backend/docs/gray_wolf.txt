Gray Wolf

Sources: This fact sheet was summarized from https://www.nps.gov/articles/yellowstone-wolf-facts.htm (National Park Service, Yellowstone National Park).

Animal class: The gray wolf, also called the timber wolf, is a mammal in the dog family.

Birth: Gray wolves give birth to live young. A female gray wolf is pregnant for about 63 days and gives birth to pups in a den in mid-April. In Yellowstone National Park, gray wolf litters average about 4 pups when they first leave the den, and the pups' eyes open at 12 to 14 days old.

Diet: Gray wolves are carnivores that also scavenge and sometimes eat small amounts of plants. In Yellowstone, elk make up most of a gray wolf's diet, along with some bison and deer. A gray wolf can eat 15 to 20 percent of its body weight in one meal.

Habitat: Gray wolves live in packs across North America, where about 67,100 to 74,100 gray wolves live. In Yellowstone National Park, a gray wolf pack's home range averages about 428 square kilometers (about 165 square miles), and packs average about 10 wolves.

Hibernation or migration: Gray wolves do not hibernate and are active all year. Young gray wolves usually leave their pack to find a new home when they are about 2 years old.

Fun facts: A gray wolf's howl can be heard up to 6.6 miles away in a forest and 9.6 miles away in open country. Gray wolves usually travel at about 5 miles an hour but can run up to 35 miles an hour. An adult male gray wolf in Yellowstone weighs about 110 pounds, and a female weighs about 90 pounds.
