Christmas Tree Worm

Sources: This fact sheet was summarized from https://aquarium.org/animals/christmas-tree-worm/ (Oregon Coast Aquarium) and https://oceana.org/marine-life/christmas-tree-worm/ (Oceana).

Animal class: The Christmas tree worm is a worm, not an insect. Christmas tree worms are polychaetes, a group of segmented worms that live in the ocean.

Birth: Christmas tree worms release eggs into the water, where the eggs are fertilized and hatch. Christmas tree worm larvae float in the ocean for about 9 to 12 days, then settle on a coral reef and build a tube on the coral.

Diet: Christmas tree worms eat phytoplankton, which are tiny plants floating in the water. Christmas tree worms catch food with two crowns of feathery parts that they also use to breathe.

Habitat: Christmas tree worms live on coral reefs around the world, from the western Indian Ocean and western Pacific to the Caribbean and eastern Atlantic. A Christmas tree worm lives in a burrow inside the coral, and only its colorful crowns stick out.

Hibernation or migration: Christmas tree worms do not migrate. Once a Christmas tree worm settles on a piece of coral, it stays there for the rest of its life.

Fun facts: Christmas tree worms are named for their two crowns, which are shaped like little Christmas trees and come in bright colors. A Christmas tree worm is about 3.8 centimeters long, shorter than most people's thumbs. When a Christmas tree worm is scared, it pulls into its burrow and closes the opening with a trapdoor. Christmas tree worms may live 10 to 40 years.
