American Black Bear

Sources: This fact sheet was summarized from https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/american-black-bear.htm (National Park Service).

Animal class: The American black bear is a mammal, and it is the smallest bear in North America.

Birth: American black bears give birth to live young. A female American black bear gives birth in her den in the middle of winter to 1 to 6 cubs, usually 2 or 3. The cubs are born blind, furry, and without teeth, and they stay with their mother for about two years.

Diet: American black bears are omnivores. Most of an American black bear's diet is berries, fruit, sedges, and insects, and it sometimes eats fish, honeycomb, or young deer and elk in spring. American black bears can smell food from more than two miles away.

Habitat: American black bears live in forests and mountains across North America, including Canada, Alaska, the Pacific Northwest, the Rocky Mountains, the upper Midwest, parts of the southern United States, the Appalachian Mountains, and Mexico.

Hibernation or migration: American black bears do not truly hibernate. In fall, American black bears build up fat and then go into a light winter sleep called torpor, when their body temperature and heart rate drop. During torpor, American black bears do not need to wake up to eat or drink, but they can wake quickly if they sense danger.

Fun facts: Despite the name, the most common color of an American black bear is brown. Each American black bear uses a home range of 15 square miles or more.
