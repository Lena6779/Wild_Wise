Sockeye Salmon

Sources: This fact sheet was summarized from https://fisheries.noaa.gov/species/sockeye-salmon (NOAA Fisheries).

Animal class: The sockeye salmon, also called the red salmon or blueback salmon, is a fish.

Birth: Sockeye salmon lay eggs. A female sockeye salmon digs a nest in the gravel with her tail, called a redd, lays 2,000 to 4,500 eggs, and covers them with gravel. Sockeye salmon eggs hatch in winter, and the babies stay in the gravel living off their yolk sacs until early spring.

Diet: Sockeye salmon are carnivores. Young sockeye salmon in fresh water eat zooplankton, tiny shrimp-like animals, and insects. In the ocean, sockeye salmon eat zooplankton, small fish, and sometimes squid.

Habitat: Sockeye salmon live along the Pacific coast of North America, from northwest Alaska to the Deschutes River in Oregon. Young sockeye salmon spend 1 to 3 years in fresh water, often in lakes, before they swim to the ocean.

Hibernation or migration: Sockeye salmon migrate. After usually 2 to 3 years in the ocean, sockeye salmon swim many miles upstream to the stream or lake where they hatched to lay their eggs. All sockeye salmon die within a few weeks after spawning, usually at about 4 or 5 years old.

Fun facts: When sockeye salmon return to spawn, their heads turn green and their bodies turn bright red. Sockeye salmon have the reddest meat of any wild salmon. Some sockeye salmon, called kokanee, never go to the ocean and spend their whole lives in fresh water. Adult sockeye salmon from the ocean are usually 1.5 to 2.5 feet long and weigh 4 to 15 pounds.
