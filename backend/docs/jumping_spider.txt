Jumping Spider

Sources: This fact sheet was summarized from https://www.livescience.com/jumping-spiders (Live Science).

Animal class: The jumping spider is an arachnid, not an insect. Jumping spiders belong to the family Salticidae, the largest family of spiders, with more than 6,380 known species.

Birth: Jumping spiders lay eggs. A female jumping spider lays a clutch of more than 100 eggs, wraps them in a silk cocoon, and guards them until they hatch.

Diet: Jumping spiders are carnivores that mostly eat small insects and smaller spiders. Jumping spiders are so good at catching flies that they are nicknamed "fly tigers." One kind of jumping spider is unusual because it eats plant leaf tips.

Habitat: Jumping spiders live on every continent except Antarctica, in forests, grasslands, scrublands, and deserts. Jumping spiders prefer the outdoors but often stay near windows and doors inside homes, where they can find prey.

Hibernation or migration: The source for this fact sheet does not say whether jumping spiders hibernate or migrate.

Fun facts: A jumping spider has four pairs of eyes, including a big front pair that works like tiny binoculars. Jumping spiders can leap up to about 6.3 inches, and they often attach a silk safety line before jumping. Male peacock spiders, a kind of jumping spider, dance and flip up their colorful bodies to impress females. Jumping spider bites are rare and usually harmless.
