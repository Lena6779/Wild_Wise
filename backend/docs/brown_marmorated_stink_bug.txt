Brown Marmorated Stink Bug

Sources: This fact sheet was summarized from https://extensionentomology.tamu.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/8/2011/05/Brown-Marmorated-Stink-Bug.pdf (Texas A&M AgriLife Extension) and https://extension.usu.edu/planthealth/ipm/notes_nuisance/stink-bug (Utah State University Extension).

Animal class: The brown marmorated stink bug is an insect, and it is a type of "true bug."

Birth: Brown marmorated stink bugs lay eggs. A female brown marmorated stink bug lays her eggs in groups of 20 to 30, side by side on the undersides of leaves, and the eggs are light yellow to yellow-red. Young brown marmorated stink bugs, called nymphs, molt five times as they grow into adults.

Diet: Brown marmorated stink bugs are herbivores that feed on many kinds of plants. Brown marmorated stink bugs eat fruits such as apples, peaches, figs, and blackberries, plus vegetables and crops such as tomatoes, beans, peppers, corn, and soybeans.

Habitat: Brown marmorated stink bugs come from China, Japan, Korea, and Taiwan. Brown marmorated stink bugs were brought to the United States by accident and were first found in Allentown, Pennsylvania, in 1998. Brown marmorated stink bugs have since been reported in many states.

Hibernation or migration: Brown marmorated stink bugs spend the winter indoors. From September to mid-October, adult brown marmorated stink bugs look for places to spend the winter, often gathering on the outside of houses and hiding in cracks. Brown marmorated stink bugs can come out again on warm, sunny winter days and in spring.

Fun facts: Brown marmorated stink bugs are named for the bad smell they give off when they are squashed. An adult brown marmorated stink bug is about 17 millimeters long, a bit more than half an inch, and is shaped like a shield. Brown marmorated stink bugs do not harm people and do not lay eggs inside homes.
