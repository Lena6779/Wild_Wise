Monarch Butterfly

Sources: This fact sheet was summarized from https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/monarch-butterfly-featured-creature.htm (National Park Service).

Animal class: The monarch butterfly is an insect.

Birth: Monarch butterflies lay eggs. A female monarch butterfly lays 300 to 500 eggs on the undersides of milkweed leaves, usually one egg per leaf. A monarch butterfly goes from egg to caterpillar to chrysalis to butterfly, which can take as few as 25 days in warm weather.

Diet: Monarch butterfly caterpillars eat only milkweed leaves, which contain a poison that makes the caterpillars and adult butterflies taste bad to predators. Adult monarch butterflies drink water and sip nectar from flowers such as thistles, daisies, sunflowers, and clover.

Habitat: Monarch butterflies live across the United States, in open places like fields, meadows, marshes, and roadsides where milkweed grows. In winter, monarch butterflies gather in mild, moist forest groves.

Hibernation or migration: Monarch butterflies migrate. Eastern monarch butterflies fly up to 3,000 miles to spend the winter in the mountains of central Mexico, while western monarch butterflies spend the winter along the California coast. The monarch butterflies that make the long trip live 6 to 9 months, much longer than the 2 to 5 weeks most adult monarchs live.

Fun facts: Migrating monarch butterflies find winter homes they have never visited before. Monarch butterfly wings are covered in tiny scales, which are flattened hairs that give the wings their color.
