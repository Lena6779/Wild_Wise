European Hedgehog

Sources: This fact sheet was summarized from https://www.rspca.org.uk/adviceandwelfare/wildlife/animals/hedgehogs (RSPCA).

Animal class: The European hedgehog, also called the West European hedgehog, is a mammal covered in spines.

Birth: European hedgehogs give birth to live young, called hoglets. A female European hedgehog is pregnant for about four weeks and usually has 4 or 5 hoglets, mostly in June and July. European hedgehog hoglets are born blind and deaf, start looking for food with their mother at about four weeks old, and are on their own by about eight weeks.

Diet: European hedgehogs mostly eat insects, such as beetles and caterpillars, plus earthworms. European hedgehogs also sometimes eat slugs, snails, small rodents, frogs, birds' eggs, and fruit. European hedgehogs cannot digest milk, so people should not feed them milk or bread.

Habitat: European hedgehogs live across most of the United Kingdom. European hedgehogs live in grasslands, farmland, woodlands, parks, and gardens, and they like hedges, bushes, and long grass for food and shelter.

Hibernation or migration: European hedgehogs hibernate. European hedgehogs usually hibernate from October or November until about March or April, in nests made from piles of leaves or logs. During hibernation, a European hedgehog's body temperature drops and its body slows down to save energy.

Fun facts: An adult European hedgehog has about 5,000 to 7,000 spines, each about 2 to 3 centimeters long. European hedgehogs can travel 1 to 2 miles in one night, and they can run, climb, and swim. European hedgehogs make many sounds, including a quacking noise when they are annoyed.
