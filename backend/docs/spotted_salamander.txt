Spotted Salamander

Sources: This fact sheet was summarized from https://www.nationalgeographic.com/animals/amphibians/facts/spotted-salamander (National Geographic) and https://portal.ct.gov/DEEP/Wildlife/Fact-Sheets/Spotted-Salamander (Connecticut Department of Energy and Environmental Protection).

Animal class: The spotted salamander is an amphibian.

Birth: Spotted salamanders lay eggs. A female spotted salamander attaches about 100 eggs to sticks or plants underwater in a pool, where they form a jelly-like mass about the size of a fist. Spotted salamander larvae hatch in about 30 days, have gills on the outside of their heads, and move onto land in late summer or early fall.

Diet: Spotted salamanders are carnivores that eat insects, worms, slugs, spiders, and millipedes.

Habitat: Spotted salamanders live in forests from eastern Canada through the eastern and midwestern United States, especially moist, low forests near rivers. Spotted salamanders spend most of their lives hidden under rocks and logs or in other animals' burrows.

Hibernation or migration: Spotted salamanders hibernate in burrows or cracks underground. When the first warm rains come in March and April, spotted salamanders travel over land on rainy nights to the pools where they lay their eggs.

Fun facts: Spotted salamanders are bluish-black with two rows of yellow or orange spots from head to tail. Spotted salamanders can release a bad-tasting, milky poison from their backs and tails to keep predators away. Spotted salamanders can live up to 20 years in the wild.
