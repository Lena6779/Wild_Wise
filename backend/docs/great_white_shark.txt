Great White Shark

Sources: This fact sheet was summarized from https://www.nationalgeographic.com/animals/fish/facts/great-white-shark (National Geographic) and https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/species/white-shark (NOAA Fisheries).

Animal class: The great white shark is a fish, and it is the largest predatory fish in the world.

Birth: Great white sharks give birth to live young instead of laying eggs. A female great white shark has a litter of 2 to 17 pups every two or three years, and each pup is about 4 to 6 feet long and can hunt on its own from birth.

Diet: Great white sharks are carnivores that eat seals, sea lions, dolphins, sea turtles, seabirds, crustaceans, and mollusks. Great white sharks also scavenge dead animals such as whale carcasses.

Habitat: Great white sharks live in cool and warm coastal waters around the world. Young great white sharks stay near coasts, while adults usually live farther out in the open ocean. In the United States, great white sharks are found from Maine to the Gulf of Mexico and from Alaska to California and Hawaii.

Hibernation or migration: Great white sharks often migrate with the seasons to stay in water of about 50 to 80 degrees Fahrenheit.

Fun facts: A great white shark has about 300 teeth in seven rows and keeps growing new ones all its life. Great white sharks can grow up to 21 feet long and weigh up to 4,500 pounds. Great white sharks can swim at almost 35 miles an hour.
