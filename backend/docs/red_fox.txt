Red Fox

Sources: This fact sheet was summarized from https://kids.nationalgeographic.com/animals/mammals/facts/red-fox (National Geographic Kids) and https://blogs.nwf.org/Educational-Resources/Wildlife-Guide/Mammals/Red-Fox (National Wildlife Federation).

Animal class: The red fox is a mammal in the dog family, related to dogs, wolves, and coyotes.

Birth: Red foxes give birth to live young, called kits or pups. Red foxes mate in winter, and the female builds a den and has a litter of 1 to 12 pups. Red fox pups are born brown or gray and usually turn red within about a month, and both parents bring them food.

Diet: Red foxes are omnivores. A red fox's favorite foods are rodents such as mice, plus rabbits, and it also eats birds, frogs, snakes, grasshoppers, and berries. Red foxes in cities sometimes eat garbage and pet food left outside.

Habitat: Red foxes are the most widespread meat-eating mammals on Earth and live across much of the Northern Hemisphere. Red foxes live near forest edges and fields, and also in cities, suburbs, backyards, and parks.

Hibernation or migration: The sources for this fact sheet do not say whether red foxes hibernate or migrate. Red foxes mostly hunt at night.

Fun facts: Red foxes make more than 20 different calls. Red foxes have excellent hearing that lets them find rodents digging underground. A group of red foxes is called a skulk or a leash. Like cats, red foxes have long whiskers and great night vision.
