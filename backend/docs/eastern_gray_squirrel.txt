Eastern Gray Squirrel

Sources: This fact sheet was summarized from https://dnr.maryland.gov/wildlife/Pages/plants_wildlife/easterngraysquirrel.aspx (Maryland Department of Natural Resources).

Animal class: The eastern gray squirrel is a mammal and a type of tree squirrel.

Birth: Eastern gray squirrels give birth to live young. A female eastern gray squirrel is pregnant for 40 to 45 days and has 2 to 6 babies that are born hairless and helpless. Eastern gray squirrels usually have two litters a year, in a tree hole or a nest made of leaves and twigs.

Diet: Eastern gray squirrels mostly eat nuts and seeds, such as acorns, hickory nuts, walnuts, beechnuts, and pine seeds, plus tree buds and berries. When food is hard to find, eastern gray squirrels also eat insects, bird eggs, young birds, and frogs or salamanders, so they are omnivores.

Habitat: Eastern gray squirrels live in hardwood forests and in suburbs and cities. Eastern gray squirrels are native to the eastern United States and southeastern Canada, from Florida north to southern Manitoba and west to eastern Texas.

Hibernation or migration: Eastern gray squirrels do not hibernate. Eastern gray squirrels stay active all winter and dig through snow to find the nuts they buried in late summer and fall.

Fun facts: An eastern gray squirrel can make hundreds of hiding spots for food and finds about 85 percent of the nuts it buries. Eastern gray squirrels can smell a buried nut under a foot of snow. Eastern gray squirrels can be gray, black, white, or blond, and they use their tails for balance, shade, warmth, and signaling.
