Earthworm

Sources: This fact sheet was summarized from https://blog.nwf.org/2014/02/ten-things-to-know-about-earthworms/ (National Wildlife Federation).

Animal class: The earthworm is a worm, not an insect. There are about 6,000 kinds of earthworms around the world, including night crawlers.

Birth: Earthworms lay eggs. Each earthworm is both male and female, and after two earthworms mate, a ring around each worm's body fills with eggs and becomes a cocoon shaped like a tiny lemon. Baby earthworms hatch tiny but fully formed and reach full size in about a year.

Diet: Earthworms eat fallen leaves and soil. As earthworms eat and dig, they move nutrients into the soil and loosen it so air can get in.

Habitat: Earthworms live in soil, where they dig burrows. More than 180 kinds of earthworms live in the United States and Canada, and about 60 of those were brought from other parts of the world.

Hibernation or migration: The source for this fact sheet does not say whether earthworms hibernate or migrate.

Fun facts: Earthworms have no lungs and breathe through their skin. Earthworms range from less than half an inch to nearly 10 feet long, though common earthworms reach about 14 inches. One Australian earthworm can squirt fluid up to 12 inches through its skin. Earthworms can live up to eight years, but most live one to two years.
